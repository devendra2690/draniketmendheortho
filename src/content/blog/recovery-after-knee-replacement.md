A knee replacement is a partnership: the surgery replaces the worn joint, but your rehabilitation decides how well it works. Here's what recovery usually looks like. Your own plan may differ.

## In hospital (days 1–3)

- Standing and walking with a walker, often on the day after surgery.
- Starting bending and straightening exercises with the physiotherapist.
- Learning to manage stairs safely before going home.

## Weeks 1–2

Focus on reducing swelling (ice, elevation) and regaining knee bend. Keep the wound clean and dry until your review.

## Weeks 3–6

Most people move from a walker to a stick. Daily exercises continue to build strength and range of motion. Many patients are comfortable with short walks and light household tasks.

## Weeks 6–12

Driving, desk work and longer walks usually become possible. Swelling and stiffness keep improving for several months.

## Warning signs — call the clinic

- Increasing redness, discharge or fever.
- Calf pain or swelling, or sudden breathlessness.
- A sudden increase in pain or inability to bear weight.
