Knee pain is one of the most common reasons people visit an orthopaedic clinic. Much of it improves with rest, activity changes and simple exercises. Knowing when to seek help can prevent a small problem from becoming a bigger one.

## Common causes

- **Osteoarthritis** — gradual wear of the joint cartilage, common after 45.
- **Ligament and meniscus injuries** — often from twisting during sport or a fall.
- **Tendon problems** — pain around the kneecap from overuse.
- **Inflammatory arthritis** — swelling in several joints, often with morning stiffness.

## See a specialist if you notice

1. Swelling that appears quickly after an injury.
2. The knee "giving way" or locking.
3. Being unable to put weight on the leg.
4. Pain that wakes you at night or lasts more than a few weeks.
5. Redness, warmth and fever — this needs **urgent** attention.

## What to expect at the consultation

Your surgeon will ask about your symptoms, examine the knee, and may request an X-ray. An MRI is needed only in selected cases. Most patients start with non-surgical treatment: strengthening exercises, weight management and pain relief.

Surgery is considered only when it clearly offers the best outcome.
